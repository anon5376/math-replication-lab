# A sharp bound for the depth-four Eliahou–Fromentin score

This note concerns Conjecture 4.3 in Eliahou and Fromentin's [*Near-misses in Wilf's conjecture*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.03623#page=12). It is **not** a disproof of Wilf's conjecture or of Conjecture 4.2 in that paper.

## Theorem

Let $S$ be a numerical semigroup with multiplicity $m$ (its least positive element), conductor $c$ (the least integer from which all integers belong to $S$), and depth $\lceil c/m\rceil=4$. Put $\rho=4m-c$, let $L=S\cap[0,c)$, and let $n$ be the number of minimal generators in $L$. Let $D_4$ be the set of elements of $[c,c+m)$ that are sums of two positive elements of $S$. The Eliahou–Fromentin score satisfies

$$
W_0(S)-\rho=n|L|-4|D_4|\ge -\binom n3-n(n-1).
$$

For each $n\ge2$, equality occurs at every $m\ge6\cdot4^{n-2}+1$. For $n=3$, equality occurs at every $m\ge13$. For $n=1$, the score is zero.

## Proof of the lower bound

Write $H=\langle m,a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1}\rangle$ for the monoid generated by the minimal generators below $c$. Every member of $L$ factors using these generators, so $L=H\cap[0,c)$. Every decomposition in $[c,c+m)$ uses only summands below $c$, since both positive summands are at least $m$. Conversely, every element of $H$ in that interval is decomposable, since none of the generators of $H$ lies there. Thus $D_4=H\cap[c,c+m)$. If $n=1$, these sets contain four and one multiples of $m$, respectively.

Assume $n\ge2$. In each residue attained by $H$ modulo $m$, take the least representative $w$. It has a factorization $w=\sum_i a_i x_i$ not using $m$. Choose the lexicographically least exponent vector among ties, and retain it only if $w<c+m$. The retained vectors form a set $K$ closed under taking coordinatewise divisors: replacing a divisor by a smaller representative of the same residue, or by a lexicographically smaller vector of the same weight, would improve the chosen vector. Each vector represents one distinct residue and yields one element of $D_4$ and
$h(x)=\lceil(c-w)/m\rceil$ elements of $L$. Here $h(x)\ge0$; the progression of elements in that residue starts at $w$ and has step $m$. At the zero vector $h=4$, and the $n-1$ unit vectors are present and have $h\ge1$ by minimality of the generators. Each upward edge in $K$ lowers $h$ by at least one since $a_i>m$. There are no vectors of total degree five or more because $w<c+m\le5m$.

Write $K_j$ for the degree-$j$ part of $K$, $x_j=|K_j|$, and $\partial X$ for the set of immediate divisors of vectors in $X$. Counting the root and unit baseline heights, then the additional thresholds forced by divisors of cubics and quartics, gives

$$
n|L|-4|D_4|
\ge n(n-1)-4x_2
   +\sum_{j=3}^4\left(n\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}|\partial^{j-i}K_j|-4x_j\right).
$$

In particular, a unit below a cubic has height at least two, and below a quartic at least three. A quadratic below a cubic has height at least one, and below a quartic at least two. These thresholds are nested because $K$ is closed under divisors, so each threshold is counted only once.

Let $N_j=\binom{n+j-2}{j}$ be the number of degree-$j$ monomials in $n-1$ variables. Choose a uniform monomial of degree $j$, then delete one of its $j$ occurrences of a variable uniformly. The result is uniform at degree $j-1$: each exponent vector $y$ of degree $j-1$ is reached with probability $\sum_i(y_i+1)/(jN_j)=1/N_{j-1}$. Therefore the fraction occupied by a shadow cannot decrease:
$|\partial^{j-i}K_j|/N_i\ge x_j/N_j$.

Using these fractions, the cubic bracket is at least $-(x_3/N_3)\binom n3$; the quartic bracket is at least $(x_4/N_4)n(n-1)(n+4)/6$. Since $x_2\le N_2=\binom n2$, the claimed lower bound follows. For $n\ge3$, equality forces all degree-two and degree-three vectors to be present and no degree-four vectors to be present. The heights must then be exactly $4,2,1,0$ by degree. Conversely, that profile attains the bound.

## Attainment

For $n\ge2$, take $a_i=m+2\cdot 4^{i-1}$ for $1\le i\le n-1$, put $c=3m+2$, and set $S=H\cup[c,\infty)$ with integer intervals. If $m\ge6\cdot4^{n-2}+1$, all the offsets $\sum_i(a_i-m)x_i$ with $\sum_i x_i\le3$ are distinct by base-four notation and lie in $[0,m)$. The generators $a_i$ lie strictly between $m$ and $2m$, making them primitive. The number $3m+1$ is missing: sums of at most two generators lie below $3m$; $3m$ is the only cubic with offset less than two; and sums of four or more generators start at $4m$. Consequently the conductor is exactly $c$. The degree-three representatives are all less than $4m$, each nonzero cubic offset is at least two, and a pure degree-four representative has weight at least $4m+8$. The vectors of degree zero through three thus give exactly the height profile $4,2,1,0$.

For $n=3$ use the smaller offsets $3,4$ instead. The offsets through degree three are $0,3,4,6,7,8,9,10,11,12$, distinct modulo every $m\ge13$. The same argument gives $(|L|,|D_4|)=(11,10)$ and score $-7$.

## Explicit disproof of the proposed minimizers

Keep $H=\langle13,16,17\rangle$ fixed. Let $S=H\cup[41,\infty)$ and $T=H\cup[45,\infty)$. Both have $m=13,\ n=3$, and depth four. Neither predecessor (40 or 44) belongs to $H$.

- For $S$, the left set is $\{0,13,16,17,26,29,30,32,33,34,39\}$. The ten decomposables in the top window are $42,43,45,46,47,48,49,50,51,52$, so the score is $-7$.
- For $T$, the left set additionally contains $42,43$. Its ten top-window decomposables are $45,46,47,48,49,50,51,52,55,56$, so the score is $-1$. Its Apéry slices in the notation of Eliahou and Fromentin are $X_1=\{16,17\}$, $X_2=\varnothing$, $X_3=\{32,33,34\}=2X_1$, and $X_4\cap D=\{48,49,50,51\}=3X_1$. The four triple sums have different residues modulo 13, so $X_1$ is a $B_3$-set modulo 13. The left primitives all lie in $S_1$ in the paper's slice notation.

Thus $T$ satisfies all three proposed minimizing conditions, but $S$ has a strictly smaller score with the same multiplicity, depth, and number of left primitives. The two usual Wilf numbers are $25$ and $33$, respectively.

## Related work

[Axel Bacher, *Families of Eliahou semigroups linked to Farey intervals*, Definition 2 and Theorem 9](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.25051v2) gives formulas for collision-free regular families that include examples attaining the bound. In particular, his parameters $h=3,\ a/b=3/2,\ \Delta=\{0,1\},\ t=2,\ m=22$ give $\langle22,31,32\rangle\cup[75,\infty)$ with score $-7$. The lower-bound argument above applies to all depth-four numerical semigroups, including ones outside those families. It establishes an exact extremum for each $n$ once $m$ is sufficiently large, not a classification of the minimum at every smaller $m$ for arbitrary $n$.
